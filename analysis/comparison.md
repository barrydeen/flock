Flock protocol - DRAFT v0.3 - 2026-06-12

# Systems comparison

This document positions Flock against the four systems it is most often compared to: Lightning, single-operator covenant-less Ark (Second's Bark, Ark Labs' Arkade), Spark (Lightspark's statechain federation), and Chaumian ecash (Cashu mints, Fedimint federations). The table is normative (it restates [synthesis §8](../research/raw/synthesis-v0.2.md) as amended by [AMENDMENTS-v0.3](../research/AMENDMENTS-v0.3.md); where they conflict, the amendments win); the prose sections are honest about where each system beats Flock. Flock's own mechanisms are specified in the [whitepaper](../whitepaper.md), [specs/onchain.md](../specs/onchain.md), and [specs/event-kinds.md](../specs/event-kinds.md); its failure modes in [attacks.md](attacks.md); its cost model in [economics.md](economics.md). External claims cite the [research digests](../research/sources.md).

## 2. Lightning

### What Lightning does better

**Payment privacy.** Lightning routes payments through onion-encrypted multi-hop paths with blinded paths for the receiver; no single routing node sees sender, receiver, and amount together. Flock concedes this dimension outright: the quorum Q must relate spends to prevent double-spending, so Q sees amounts and payment links at cosign time ([synthesis §9.1](../research/raw/synthesis-v0.2.md)). Blinded cosigning compatible with fraud proofs is an open problem. Flock's privacy floor is "public sees only commitments and nullifiers; operators see the graph" — strictly worse than Lightning's against the operators themselves.

**Trust model purity.** A Lightning channel involves no operator at all — only your counterparty, and the penalty mechanism makes even that adversarial-safe for confirmed state. Flock's checkpointed funds are theft-proof so long as the owner or its towers act within the stated deadlines; uncheckpointed arkoor receipts are revocable by the sender *alone* if the receiver stays offline beyond the ladder differential (144–576 blocks, by hop) after a hostile unroll and no tower acts, revocable by sender + 4-of-7 collusion otherwise, and extinguished at checkpoint. Lightning has no analogous window.

**Payee finality.** A settled Lightning HTLC is final at preimage reveal; nothing short of a successful channel breach claws it back. Flock's arkoor receipt — 1–2 s as a target pending measurement over relay+Tor paths — is collusion-bounded until the next checkpoint, and an uncheckpointed receipt is not *accepted* until the receiver's fail-closed wake-up verification passes. A sender clawing back value before acceptance is a failed payment, not theft of accepted funds, but the window exists and Lightning has none: arkoor payee finality is weaker than Lightning's. Flock accepts this because the same structure buys offline receive and zero inbound liquidity; merchants needing Lightning-grade finality must require checkpoint inclusion or verify online (including deposit existence).

**Network effects and interop.** Lightning is accepted across exchanges and processors — Coinbase routed ~15% of BTC withdrawals over it in mid-2025 ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/why-lightning-network-capacity-declining-20-in-2025-is-not-as-bad-as-it-sounds/)) — with four interoperable implementations, BOLT11/BOLT12/LNURL/BIP-353 addressing, and a mature LSP spec track (bLIP-50/51/52, [github.com/BitcoinAndLightningLayerSpecs/lsp](https://github.com/BitcoinAndLightningLayerSpecs/lsp)). Flock has none of this yet; it is a design.

**Amount granularity.** Lightning settles to the millisatoshi. Flock's minimum leaf denomination is 10,000 sats, and sub-floor change rides uncheckpointed.

### What Flock does better

**Receiving.** A new Lightning user cannot receive a single sat until someone commits capital toward them; inbound liquidity is architectural, not incidental ([lightning.engineering](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/liquidity/how-to-get-inbound-capacity-on-the-lightning-network)). A new Flock user receives instantly to an npub with zero on-chain setup and no liquidity provisioning; rounds front capital pool-wide rather than per-edge.

**Offline receive.** Noncustodial mobile receive on Lightning in 2026 still means an LSP holding the HTLC and hoping a push notification wakes the phone; the async-payments spec remains unmerged ([bitcoinops.org/en/topics/async-payments](https://bitcoinops.org/en/topics/async-payments/)). Flock receivers need no liveness to be paid: the gift-wrapped package waits on relays, sender-funded watchtower deposits (height- *and* conflict-triggered) defend it against stale-owner unrolls and toward expiry, and standing tower deposits exit checkpointed value at T_exp+288. Two honest caveats: the receipt is not *accepted* until the receiver's wake-up verification passes, and the offline defense makes a tower economy load-bearing — the watchtower critique usually aimed at Lightning applies to Flock too, though Flock gives it an explicit funding path (a contingent ~2,000-sat fee output riding each pre-signed escalating bump child — 10/50/200 sat/vB, funded by the owner's dedicated fee UTXOs — paid at most once and only if a tower acts).

**No toxic state.** Any stale copy of Lightning channel state can cost the entire channel, and static channel backups depend on the peer being honest, reachable, and running ([lnd safety.md](https://github.com/lightningnetwork/lnd/blob/master/docs/safety.md)). Flock's stale vaults are harmless — exiting an already-forfeited leaf only loses an already-spent coin — and checkpointed funds are recoverable via the recovery tapleaf from the seed plus any surviving copy of the public round data (public, replicated, non-secret — but a nonzero availability assumption, unlike Lightning's secret toxic state).

**Bounded exit cost.** A Lightning force-close broadcasts one transaction per in-flight HTLC; with full HTLC slots at 50 sat/vB it can exceed 1M sats ([Voltage](https://voltage.cloud/blog/lightning-node-management-handling-high-on-chain-fees)). Comparing at the same feerates: Flock's unilateral exit is pre-signed and fixed-shape, independent of payment activity — the first exiter pays ≈1.6 kvB (~16k sats at 10 sat/vB, ~80k at 50), the marginal exiter ≈310 vB incl. its CPFP child (~3.1k sats at 10 sat/vB, ~15.5k at 50). Flock's worst case is bounded where Lightning's scales with in-flight HTLCs; a calm-mempool Lightning close (one small transaction) remains cheaper than a Flock first exit at any given feerate.

**No single-node points of failure.** Lightning's public capacity concentrated to ~62% in the top 10 operators by August 2025 ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/data-shows-sustained-slide-in-lightning-network-capacity-channels-through-2025/)); each hub is a unilateral liveness dependency for its spokes. Flock's quorum survives 3 of 7 operators down, and total quorum death still leaves every user a consensus-ordered exit via the claim ladder (ordered by maturity; defense against stale claims still requires someone to act in time — a held forfeit broadcast within the 144–720-block forfeit stale window, or a ladder defense landed before the attacker's claim matures, 288–720 blocks after the unroll).

**Jamming.** Lightning's free-to-fail HTLC slots remain exploitable; the hybrid mitigation is not yet network-wide ([bitcoinops.org/en/topics/channel-jamming-attacks](https://bitcoinops.org/en/topics/channel-jamming-attacks/)). Flock's equivalents (round seats, refresh intents) are bonded and PoW-gated by construction (1,000-sat default intent bond; PoW ≥ 20 bits, demand capped at 20), though grief-vs-loss indistinguishability remains conceded.

### Migration and interop

Flock does not require abandoning Lightning. Three bridges:

- **LN gateways.** Kind-38406 offer books advertise vHTLC/PTLC swap providers bridging Flock vTXOs to BOLT11/BOLT12 invoices at 0.1–0.5% swap fees, so a Flock wallet can pay any Lightning invoice and receive from any Lightning sender from day one.
- **LSP pivot.** An existing LSP's balance sheet maps directly onto operating a Flock pool or gateway: a recycling float of ≈ hot balance × 58 d / refresh cycle, since fronted capital stays locked from cell funding to root sweep ~58 days per refresh (≈12.6 BTC for 10k users at a 100k-sat average, 50% hot, 23-day cycle — [economics.md](economics.md)), with nothing locked per-edge. Lightning's own LSP economics already concentrate capital this way; Flock prices it explicitly through the 0.5–0.9%-per-refresh band, sold ≈ at cost — the margin lives in arkoor flow fees, gateway swaps, and pinning subscriptions.
- **Zap compatibility.** Flock emits standard NIP-57 kind-9735 zap receipts, so existing Nostr social clients display Flock payments without modification.

## 3. Single-operator Ark (Bark / Arkade)

Flock *is* a covenant-less Ark at the mechanism level — vTXO trees, hourly rounds, connector-bound forfeits, arkoor transfers — so this comparison is the narrowest.

### What single-operator Ark does better

**Simplicity and maturity.** One coordinator means no DKG, no proactive resharing, no intra-quorum FROST transport, no two-signature tapscripts (Flock pays ~+70 vB per quorum-gated input for removing the FROST/MuSig2 composition). Second's Bark launched on mainnet June 9, 2026 ([Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/news/second-launches-bark-on-bitcoin-mainnet)) and Arkade's public beta in October 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/375271/ark-labs-arkade-public-beta-layer-2-bitcoin)); Flock is unimplemented. A single operator also makes round ceremonies and fee policy operationally simpler, and a tightly-run gRPC API ([docs.arklabs.xyz](https://docs.arklabs.xyz/ark/)) is easier to keep live than 8+ public relays plus a 7-party quorum.

### What Flock does better

**Operator failure.** The single Ark operator is a SPOF for rounds, arkoor cosigning, and liquidity; if it halts, users must exit on-chain before expiry ([spark.money](https://www.spark.money/research/ark-protocol-explained)). Flock tolerates 3 operators down with no user impact, and a fully dead quorum degrades to exit-only mode with ladder-ordered claims rather than a race.

**Expiry confiscation.** In deployed Ark designs, missing every round for ~30 days lets the server sweep your coins ([bitcoinops.org/en/topics/ark](https://bitcoinops.org/en/topics/ark/)); the trustless fix (Erk) needs CTV+CSFS ([roose.io](https://roose.io/blog/hark-erk-evolving-the-ark-protocol-using-ctv-and-csfs/)). Flock's recovery tapleaf matures at T_exp+288, 3,744 blocks (~26 days) before the quorum sweep at T_exp+4032, and every checkpoint lodges a standing tower deposit that exits a dormant wallet at recovery maturity. Dormancy therefore degrades to *tower-protected* self-custody: funds are not confiscated while towers function, but the exit is automatic and forced — ~32 days after the last checkpoint, the standing deposit exits them to on-chain self-custody at the owner's expense (≈3.1k–16k sats at 10 sat/vB, 5× at 50, plus the ~2,000-sat contingent tower fee) — and if the owner stays absent *and* no tower acts within the ~26-day window, the quorum sweep confiscates. So dormant balances pay no carrying fee until that one exit, versus Ark's forced ~0.5%/month refresh-or-sweep treadmill — though note that Flock's *actively refreshed* balances pay a comparable 0.5–0.9% per refresh, roughly every 23 days at the deadline-driven cadence ([economics.md](economics.md)).

**Stale-exit defense without a live server.** Bark's exits carry a ~10-hour CSV so the live server can broadcast a forfeit against a malicious exit ([second.tech/docs/learn/forfeits](https://second.tech/docs/learn/forfeits)); Flock's decrementing claim ladder (720 → 144 by hops of 144) is consensus-ordered and needs no live quorum — but ordering is maturity, not preclusion: a stale owner's claim stays valid forever, so someone must defend after a hostile unroll — a refresh-stale claim is answered by broadcasting the held forfeit within the **forfeit stale window** (144–720 blocks, by the stale owner's hop), and a stale prior owner attacking an uncheckpointed chain must be answered before its claim matures, 288–720 blocks after the unroll (the current owner's head start is 144–576 blocks). Conflict-triggered tower deposits cover the offline case; the live server is replaced by a tower market, not by nothing. Bark's hash-lock refresh atomicity is replaced by connector outputs with nothing to withhold.

**Open transport.** All Flock coordination is signed Nostr events over public relays — auditable, multi-homed, censorship-evadable — rather than a proprietary operator API that is itself a liveness and censorship chokepoint. Scope: no operator endpoint is load-bearing for checkpointed funds; uncheckpointed receipts additionally depend on relay retention, including the operator-run archival relays (plain NIP-01) listed in the pool advertisement.

The trust *window* is identical in shape: in both systems an arkoor receipt is revocable by sender-plus-operator(s) until refresh ([second.tech/docs/learn/payments](https://second.tech/docs/learn/payments)), and by the sender *alone* against a receiver who stays offline past the ladder differential after a hostile unroll — Flock mitigates that case with conflict-triggered tower deposits and treats any receipt as unaccepted until wake-up verification. Flock widens the collusion requirement from 1 to 4-of-7 and bounds the exposure with the 50,000-sat value-at-risk auto-checkpoint (enforced while online; offline exposure is bounded by the acceptance rule).

## 4. Spark

### What Spark does better

**Carrying cost.** Spark transfers move key material — the operator group additively tweaks its share so the combined key is preserved ([docs.spark.money/learn/transfers](https://docs.spark.money/learn/transfers)) — so no operator capital is fronted per transfer and there is no ~30-day expiry forcing fee-bearing refreshes. Flock's 0.5–0.9% of refreshed value per refresh (break-even ≈0.8% per refresh at 5% APY: each refresh locks fronted capital until the root sweep, ~58 days, so float ≈ hot balance × 58 d / refresh cycle — [economics.md](economics.md)) — ≈0.5–0.9% per ~23-day cycle for a deadline-driven casual user, proportionally more at higher velocity — is structural to covenant-less Ark capital recycling; Spark simply does not pay it. Spark's on-chain footprint per user is also lower.

**Shipped Lightning interop and adoption.** Spark receives Lightning payments offline via operator-held preimage shards ([docs.spark.money/learn/lightning](https://docs.spark.money/learn/lightning)) and has real deployment: Breez SDK, Wallet of Satoshi, Xverse (~1.6M users), a regulated native stablecoin ([lightspark.com](https://www.lightspark.com/news/spark/breez-spark-partnership), [coinedition.com](https://coinedition.com/xverse-integrates-spark-unlocking-instant-bitcoin-defi-and-stablecoins-for-1-6-million-users/)).

### What Flock does better

**No third-party key-deletion assumption on settled funds.** Spark's safety rests on operators provably-unprovably forgetting old key shares; retention plus a past owner equals theft, and "users are unable to have any finality assurances" ([bitcoinlayers.org/layers/spark](https://www.bitcoinlayers.org/layers/spark), [Shinobi's "Trustodial" analysis](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/technical/trustodial-an-ontological-dilemma)). Flock's only deletion assumption on settled funds is the user deleting their *own* ephemeral tree key — self-enforced, not delegated — and checkpointed vTXOs are theft-proof against full operator collusion so long as the owner or its towers act within the stated deadlines. To hold Flock to the standard it applies to Spark: operator share deletion is equally unverifiable in Flock — kind-31401 attestations carry reputational and legal weight only — but retained quorum shares cannot reach checkpointed funds, only the residual-trust window. That residual (uncheckpointed arkoor: revocable by the sender alone against an offline receiver if no tower acts, by sender + 4-of-7 otherwise) is time-bounded and value-capped; Spark's is permanent for any leaf whose share history was retained.

**Operator-set diversity.** As of 2025–26 Spark has two signing operators, Lightspark and Flashnet ([panewslab.com](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/4uznhoso)), and the service-provider role is closed ("no clear way to run an SSP without a business relationship with Lightspark," [issue #64](https://github.com/buildonspark/spark/issues/64)). Flock specifies 7 independent organizations across diverse ASNs and jurisdictions, with permissionless watchtowers and gateways.

**Privacy against the operator set is comparable — but not addressing.** Both systems let operators see the payment graph. Spark additionally ships static addresses that critics call "worse than on-chain," with one operator publishing all transactions ([Seth For Privacy](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/technical/spark-and-ark-a-look-at-our-newest-bitcoin-layer-twos)); Flock uses per-payment stealth tweaks and a commitments-only public checkpoint log.

**Seed-only recovery.** Spark's is partial; Flock recovers all checkpointed funds from the seed plus any surviving copy of the public round data — 38402 round results held by relays, vaults, towers, archival relays, or any cell peer: public, replicated, non-secret data, but a nonzero availability assumption (conceded: uncheckpointed receipts are not seed-recoverable in either system — see [attacks.md](attacks.md)).

## 5. Cashu and Fedimint

### What ecash does better

**Privacy.** Chaumian blind signatures mean the mint cannot link issuance to redemption; this is categorically better than Flock, Spark, Ark, or Lightning. The NIP-60/61 ecosystem also demonstrates clean Nostr-native UX — wallet state on relays, nutzaps where "the payment itself is the receipt" ([NIP-60](https://nips.nostr.com/60), [NIP-61](https://nips.nostr.com/61)).

**Cost and footprint.** Intra-mint transfers are effectively free and touch the chain not at all. For sub-dust micropayments this is unbeatable; Flock's 10,000-sat leaf floor and 1–3 sat + 0.05% arkoor fee cannot compete at that scale.

### What Flock does better

Everything custodial. The mint or federation holds the coins: it can be drained, rugged, inflated (unverifiable issuance), or seized, and there is no unilateral exit — Fedimint distributes this custody across a federation but does not eliminate it. Token loss is also bearer-asset loss: the NIP-60 spec itself accepts that losing the relay events means losing the money ([NIP-60](https://nips.nostr.com/60)). Flock's checkpointed funds are non-custodial with a pre-signed, ladder-ordered exit that no operator coalition can block, and dormant funds pay no carrying fee and are not confiscated while towers function — though unlike a mint balance, which sits for free indefinitely (at full custodial risk), they are automatically exited to on-chain self-custody ~32 days after the last checkpoint at the owner's expense (~5–18k sats; confiscation only if owner and all towers fail to act for ~26 days after recovery maturity). The honest framing: ecash is the right tool *below* Flock's denomination floor and for privacy-critical spending; Flock is the right tool for balances you cannot afford to have custodied.

## 6. When NOT to use Flock

- **Sub-dust micropayments.** The minimum leaf is 10,000 sats; value near the ~310 vB exit cost is economically strandable on demand, and sub-floor change rides uncheckpointed. Use ecash (possibly a Cashu mint funded *from* Flock via an LN gateway) for sat-scale payments.
- **Privacy-critical payments.** Q sees amounts and payment links at cosign time; this is conceded as below Lightning's onion-routed level and far below blind ecash. If operator-visible graph metadata is unacceptable, do not route the payment through Flock.
- **Pure savings beyond what dormancy serves.** Dormant balances pay no carrying fee, but ~32 days after the last checkpoint the standing tower deposit automatically exits them to on-chain self-custody at the owner's expense (≈3.1k–16k sats at 10 sat/vB, 5× at 50, plus the ~2,000-sat contingent tower fee) — a leaf near the 10,000-sat floor is substantially consumed by its own exit. The guarantee rides on the tower market, and if the owner stays absent and no tower acts for ~26 days after recovery maturity, the quorum sweep confiscates. Savings-profile pools can stretch expiry and sweep_delay to lengthen the dormancy horizon; actively refreshed balances pay 0.5–0.9% per refresh (~every 23 days). For long-horizon cold storage, plain L1 UTXOs are strictly simpler and carry no such deadline or exit cost; Flock's machinery buys payment capability you would not be using.
- **High-velocity balances kept in-pool.** The refresh fee is per refresh: a once-a-cycle casual user pays ~0.5–0.9% per ~23-day cycle, but hop-4 velocity forces checkpoints far more often, multiplying the cost proportionally. Merchants at that velocity should sweep revenue out through an LN gateway or offboard on-chain rather than pay high-frequency refreshes ([economics.md](economics.md)).
- **Jurisdictions where 4-of-7 diverse operators cannot exist.** The trust model assumes independent organizations across ASNs and jurisdictions. Where one legal authority can compel 4 of 7 operators, theft of uncheckpointed funds and graph surveillance collapse toward the single-operator model; only the unilateral exit and claim ladder survive. Users in such environments should treat Flock as exit-guaranteed but surveilled, and size their value-at-risk accordingly.
- **Today.** Flock is a design with no implementation, no audits, and an unproven watchtower market. Lightning, Bark, Arkade, Spark, and Cashu all exist; anyone needing to move sats this year should use them.
