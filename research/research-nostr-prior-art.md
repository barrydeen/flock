# Nostr as Payments/Coordination Infrastructure (as of 2026-06-12)

## HALF A — Prior Art

### Payments primitives (mature)
- **NIP-47 Nostr Wallet Connect** — remote wallet control over relays (encrypted request/response events). De-facto standard for app↔wallet links; Alby Hub ships it as core (v1.21.5, early 2026, added a second default relay for reliability) ([nips/47.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/47.md), [Alby guide](https://guides.getalby.com/developer-guide/nostr-wallet-connect-api)). Encryption migrated NIP-04→NIP-44; extension methods (pay_keysend, list_transactions, etc.) added in NWC v0.4.1 ([nobsbitcoin](https://www.nobsbitcoin.com/nwc-v0-4-1/)). Proof that request/response RPC over relays works in production for money.
- **NIP-57 zaps** — Lightning payments attested by kind-9735 receipts signed by the recipient's LNURL server; trust rests on that server, not relays ([nips.nostr.com/57 via index](https://nips.nostr.com/)). Ubiquitous since 2023.
- **NIP-60/61 Cashu wallets & nutzaps** — wallet *state stored on relays* (encrypted token events, kind 7375/17375) for cross-app portability; nutzaps (kind 9321) are P2PK-locked Cashu tokens where "the payment itself is the receipt" ([NIP-60](https://nips.nostr.com/60), [NIP-61](https://nips.nostr.com/61), [PR #1369](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/1369)). This is the strongest existing precedent for relays as a *bearer-asset data-availability layer* — losing the events means losing money, and the spec accepts that risk. Still evolving (e.g., [PR #1890](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/1890), 2025).
- **NIP-87 mint discovery** — kinds 38172/38173/38000 for discovering/recommending Cashu mints ([search index](https://nips.nostr.com/)).

### Coordination protocols over relays
- **FROSTR** — FROST threshold Schnorr with *relays as the signing-round transport*; t-of-n shares produce standard BIP-340 sigs. Active 2025–26: Bifrost library, Igloo desktop/web/iOS, frost2x extension; OpenSats-funded with roadmap including **`sign_psbt` for "interactive bitcoin p2p protocols over nostr"** ([FROSTR-ORG](https://github.com/FROSTR-ORG/), [OpenSats 12th wave](https://opensats.org/blog/twelfth-wave-of-nostr-grants), [bifrost](https://github.com/frostr-org/bifrost)). Demonstrates multi-round interactive crypto protocols over relays.
- **Mostro** — non-custodial LN P2P exchange; daemon coordinates trades via gift-wrapped DMs; order book as addressable kind-38383 events under **NIP-69** (merged Nov 2024, shared liquidity with RoboSats). Production-ready, active releases through 2026, Android client shipping ([mostro.network](https://mostro.network/), [NIP-69](https://nips.nostr.com/69), [repo](https://github.com/MostroP2P/mostro)).
- **Civkit** — P2P marketplace paper (Riard/Gregory) + node with order book, escrow, PGP chat; alpha mid-2024, explicitly "not production-ready"; little visible progress since — **status likely stale** ([civkit.github.io](https://civkit.github.io/), [nobsbitcoin alpha](https://www.nobsbitcoin.com/civkit-v1-alpha-released/)).
- **joinstr** — coinjoin coordination via relays (output registration, PSBT exchange per pool, ~5 participants), no central coordinator/fidelity bonds; mitigates DoS via encrypted per-round channels and recommends *paid/private relays*; Android app v0.1.1 with mainnet support ([joinstr.xyz](https://joinstr.xyz/), [docs](https://docs.joinstr.xyz), [nobsbitcoin](https://www.nobsbitcoin.com/joinstr-app-v0-1-1/)).
- **Smart Vaults (ex-Coinstr)** — miniscript policies + PSBT signature orchestration entirely over relays ([repo](https://github.com/smartvaults/smartvaults)); **Munstr** — MuSig signing rounds over relays, PoC ([nobsbitcoin](https://www.nobsbitcoin.com/munstr-musig-nostr/)). Both appear dormant (2023-era) — verify before citing as live.
- **NIP-88 DLCs over Nostr** ([PR #919](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/919), benthecarman) — oracle announcements/attestations + contract negotiation as events; implemented in DlcDevKit's Nostr oracle marketplace and Ernest oracle ([benschroth.com](https://benschroth.com/blog/dlcdevkit-beyond/)); Mutiny's Note Duel built on it (Mutiny since shut down — older material).
- **bitcoin-nostr-relay** — relaying raw Bitcoin txs over Nostr when P2P propagation fails ([vnprc repo](https://github.com/vnprc/bitcoin-nostr-relay)).

### Direct answer: has a Bitcoin L2 on Nostr coordination/DA been proposed/built? **Yes — at least once, very recently and early-stage.**
- **OpenArk / BOLT-ARK ("NIP-150")** — community spec (no company/token) for an *Ark-style L2 using Nostr relays as the standardized transport and coordination layer*: every ASP runs its own relay; message types, transaction templates, key derivation, and relay coordination are specified; positioned explicitly against Ark Labs'/Second's proprietary gRPC APIs ("standardized, auditable transport"). Roadmap: public testnet Q2 2026, mainnet beta Q3 2026, audits Q4 2026 — i.e., **spec published, implementation in development, nothing live** ([openark.tech](https://openark.tech/)). Caveats: low visibility (website-first; GitHub presence hard to surface in search), and "NIP-150" is a self-assigned number — it is **not** in the canonical [nostr-protocol/nips](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips) repo. Treat as unproven prior art, but it is conceptual prior art for "Ark over Nostr transport."
- Mainstream Ark implementations (Ark Labs/Arkade, Second/bark) use their own client-server APIs, **not** Nostr ([docs.arklabs.xyz](https://docs.arklabs.xyz/ark/)). No statechain-over-Nostr or channel-factory-over-Nostr project surfaced in searches; nearest neighbors are NIP-88 DLCs, FROSTR's planned sign_psbt, and the NIP-60/61 relays-as-DA precedent. The specific idea space is thus **occupied but barely** — one early spec, no shipped system.

## HALF B — Relay Network Properties as Infrastructure

- **Retention is discretionary.** Relays can't alter signed events but choose what to store and for how long; free relays purge aggressively, paid relays sell longer retention/uptime ([voltage.cloud](https://voltage.cloud/blog/understanding-nostr-data-storage-relays-and-decentralization), [nostr.co.uk](https://nostr.co.uk/learn/nostr-relays-explained/)). No relay offers contractual durability; an L2 needing guaranteed DA must run its own relay(s) — exactly OpenArk's "ASP-operated relay" choice.
- **Paid relays / economics.** Typical premium: nostr.wine ~21k sats (~$7)/mo with NIP-42 auth, regional mirrors, search; WoT filter relays ~10k sats/mo ([docs.nostr.wine](https://docs.nostr.wine/filter/readme)). One 2025 analysis claims ~95% of relays don't cover costs ([dev.to](https://dev.to/jonathan_greenallidoizc/95-of-nostr-relays-cant-cover-costs-heres-why-that-matters-189l)) — relay churn is a real DA risk.
- **NIP-65 outbox** — kind-10002 read/write relay lists; clients fetch from authors' write relays, deliver to recipients' read relays. Works for social broadcast; known gaps for context-specific traffic (DMs, groups) and divergent client selection algorithms ([NIP-65 PR](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/pull/218), [JeffG on gossip](https://habla.news/jeffg/1711468354646), [relay-type issue #1282](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/issues/1282)).
- **Ephemeral events** (kinds 20000–29999, NIP-01): relayed but not stored — suited to signing-round/coordination chatter, useless for DA.
- **NIP-40 expiration** — relays MAY delete after the expiration tag; best-effort only, clients must not rely on it ([nips.nostr.com/40 via index](https://nips.nostr.com/)). Useful hygiene for offers/quotes (Mostro uses expiration semantics).
- **NIP-13 PoW** — leading-zero-bit difficulty in event id; 16–20 bits ≈ <1 s on consumer hardware; raises spam cost without identity ([nips/13.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/13.md)).
- **NIP-42 AUTH** — challenge/response auth; enables paid/whitelisted writes ("auth-required:", "restricted:") and recipient-gated reads of DMs/gift wraps ([nips/42.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/42.md)).
- **Spam history & rate limits.** The 2024 "ReplyGuy" bot wave (disposable pubkeys, duplicate content) pushed relays toward PoW, AUTH, WoT filters, and duplicate-content checks ([nosflare](https://github.com/Spl0itable/nosflare)); per-IP/per-pubkey rate limits are ubiquitous but unstandardized (advertised via NIP-11 limits, [nips.nostr.com/11](https://nips.nostr.com/11)). Plan for writes being silently dropped or "restricted."
- **Blossom** — sha256-addressed blob storage on HTTP "mediaservers" authed by Nostr keys; BUDs cover mirroring/metadata; default in Primal, integrated in Amethyst etc.; user-relocatable blobs ([hzrd149/blossom](https://github.com/hzrd149/blossom), [NIP-B7](https://nips.nostr.com/B7)). Natural home for bulk L2 data (e.g., VTXO trees) with hashes on relays.
- **NIP-59 gift wrap privacy** — rumor→seal→wrap with one-time keys hides sender, content, kind; timestamps randomized into the past against time-analysis ([nips/59.md](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/59.md)). Limits: recipient pubkey visible in the wrap's p-tag (relays SHOULD AUTH-gate kind 1059, many don't); IP-level metadata unprotected; some relays decline to store gift wraps at all.
- **Delivery reliability.** Per-relay OK acks exist but mean only "this relay accepted it." Empirical baseline (Feb 2024 measurement — older data, network has grown): avg post replicated on ~34.6 relays; >90% of posts remain retrievable even with the top-30 relays down; 98.2% of multi-relay retrievals redundant ([arXiv 2402.05709](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05709)). Practical expectation: publishing to 5–10 independent relays gives high probability that ≥1 retains the event short-term, but **no relay guarantees retention or ordering**; protocols needing consistency must treat relays as lossy broadcast plus self-hosted authoritative storage (the NWC second-relay and joinstr paid-relay patterns both reflect this).

**Bottom line:** every building block (wallet RPC, bearer-token DA, threshold signing, trade/coinjoin coordination, DLC negotiation) already runs over relays; a full Bitcoin L2 on Nostr coordination exists only as the unproven, early-2026 OpenArk/BOLT-ARK spec — the design space is validated but effectively unclaimed.
